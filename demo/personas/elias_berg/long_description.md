# Elias Berg: long description

> Synthetic Relay demo persona. No real person is represented.

Elias is a 38-year-old community-program designer living in Malmö. He works four days a week for a municipal housing foundation, where he helps tenant groups turn underused courtyards and ground-floor rooms into shared workshops, repair cafés, gardens, and after-school spaces. He is good at starting with the people who already care instead of announcing a polished program and hoping attendance follows.

His professional life is social, but his private life has become narrower than he wants. Several close friends moved away after having children, and Elias noticed that most of his new relationships remained tied to a project or meeting. He would like two or three friendships that survive cancelled events and do not require a formal reason to meet. He enjoys long walks, cooking for a small group, second-hand furniture restoration, cold-water swimming, and unhurried conversations about cities. He does not enjoy high-volume networking or nightlife built around drinking.

Elias is also considering a small business with the right partner. The idea is a kit and service for housing associations that want resident-run repair rooms but lack operating rules, supplier relationships, insurance templates, and a realistic volunteer rota. He has tested parts of the model through his day job but has not formed a company, taken payment, or decided whether it should be a cooperative. He wants to meet someone with commercial operations experience who respects community governance. He does not want an investor-first business or a partner who treats volunteer labor as free supply.

He writes in a patient, observational voice. He distrusts heroic founder stories and claims that one app can “fix community.” His strongest stories begin with a concrete scene: six broken lamps on a table, three languages spoken in a workshop, or the quiet half hour when only one resident arrives. He is comfortable admitting when an event failed. He believes good community work makes participation easier without pretending that everyone wants the same level of involvement.

Elias speaks Swedish and English and can manage basic Danish. He grew up outside Växjö and moved to Malmö for university. He shares an apartment with an elderly rescue cat named Siv. He cycles almost everywhere but has no interest in cycling as an identity. His schedule is flexible on Friday afternoons and Sundays. He prefers meeting locally before committing to a recurring collaboration.

## What Elias hopes Relay can find

- A Malmö-area friend interested in cooking, urban walks, repair, or neighborhood life.
- A practical business partner with experience in cooperative operations, facilities, insurance, or B2B services.
- People who have operated shared workshops and can exchange honest lessons.

## Boundaries and uncertainties

- Public: city, work themes, hobbies, business idea at a high level.
- Keep private: home address, personal email, family details, and his employer’s unannounced projects.
- Ask before presenting him as actively fundraising or recruiting a cofounder; he currently prefers “exploring a business partner.”
- Do not turn friendship discovery into dating language.
