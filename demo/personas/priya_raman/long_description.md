# Priya Raman: working style and personal context

> Synthetic Relay demo persona. No real person is represented.

Priya is a staff engineer who is good at finding the organizational problem hiding inside a technical request. When a team asks for a faster build, she wants to know whether the build is slow, whether developers cannot predict when it will be slow, or whether nobody trusts the cache enough to investigate. She likes infrastructure but dislikes infrastructure mystique.

For several years she has kept a notebook of developer-tool ideas. The one she returns to is a local “change impact” assistant for medium-sized engineering teams: before opening a pull request, it would explain which services, owners, tests, and operational assumptions a change touches. She thinks current products either require a platform organization to maintain a graph or produce vague AI summaries. Her hypothesis is that repository history, ownership files, and CI behavior can support a narrower useful product.

Priya does not want to start a company alone. She wants a product-oriented cofounder who can interview engineering leaders without turning every conversation into validation, sell a small pilot, and challenge scope. She expects to keep her job during early discovery. She can contribute evenings and one weekend day for three months before making a larger decision. She will not use proprietary employer data or recruit coworkers into an undisclosed side project.

Outside work, Priya wants more friendships that are not extensions of the Toronto tech scene. She enjoys ceramics, science-fiction novels, women’s cricket, neighborhood restaurants, and walking through the ravines after fresh snow. She is friendly in groups but rarely turns a good group conversation into a one-to-one friendship. An agent could help by proposing low-pressure, specific reasons to meet again.

Her communication is direct and dry. She dislikes exaggerated certainty and sentences that could appear on any startup website. She often uses a small technical example and then names the organizational consequence. She is generous with detailed feedback but needs collaborators to say what kind of feedback they want. She prefers a written outline before a consequential meeting and makes decisions after sleeping on them.

## Good matches

- A product/commercial cofounder with firsthand knowledge of engineering-management pain.
- A technical peer who has built code intelligence, CI, or repository analysis systems and wants to compare approaches.
- A Toronto friend interested in ceramics, speculative fiction, cricket, or long winter walks.

## Friction points

- Avoid people selling a general autonomous coding platform; her interest is a bounded pre-PR workflow.
- She will disengage from manufactured urgency, vague equity promises, or requests for free architecture work.
- Do not interpret concise replies as disinterest.
- Ask before connecting her to current Northline employees or customers.
