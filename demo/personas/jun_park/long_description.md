# Jun Park: long description

> Synthetic Relay demo persona. No real person is represented.

Jun is a 34-year-old Korean service designer based in Berlin, with three or four extended stays in Seoul each year. They work independently on public services, mostly healthcare access, residency processes, and multilingual casework. Jun’s strongest skill is noticing where an official process assumes one person has stable time, language, health, internet access, and emotional capacity. Their work makes room for helpers without erasing the person whose case it is.

Jun studied visual communication, moved into user research, and spent four years at a civic-design consultancy before going independent. They are considering a small studio rather than growing a conventional agency. The studio would take on fewer, longer projects and stay through implementation. Jun could lead research, service mapping, prototyping, and facilitation. They need a partner who understands institutional sales, procurement, and delivery inside healthcare or government. They are wary of consultancies that sell senior attention and staff projects with juniors.

The cofounder question is open. Jun might prefer a business partner with an equal but different discipline, or a networked studio with no single cofounder. An agent should surface both options and never announce that Jun is “launching a company.” A useful connection would begin with comparing working models or collaborating on one proposal.

Jun also wants a few deeper friendships in Berlin. Their social calendar looks full, but much of it consists of professional openings, community dinners, and friends-of-friends. Jun enjoys printmaking, quiet electronic music, Korean home cooking, swimming, and long conversations in cafés where nobody is waiting for the table. They do not want romantic matching. They would appreciate friends who can make plans one week ahead and who are comfortable with periods when Jun is in Seoul.

Jun writes in a reflective but concrete voice. They dislike certainty that erases institutional complexity. They will criticize a system without treating the people inside it as stupid. They often move between a small personal scene and a structural observation. Their work sometimes touches family illness and immigration; the agent must not publish details beyond what Jun has already chosen to make public.

## Desired connections

- A Berlin friend interested in print, music, swimming, cooking, or public life.
- A potential studio partner with public-sector procurement or healthcare delivery experience.
- Researchers and operators working on delegated access, multilingual casework, and high-stakes service transitions.
