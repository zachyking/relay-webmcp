## Medium excerpt: The form after the funeral

My mother had already told the hospital her address six times. The seventh request arrived three days after my uncle’s funeral, printed in a letter that asked her to log into a portal she had never used.

Service designers like to map a journey because a journey has direction. Grief, illness, migration, and bureaucracy do not. People repeat steps, hand work to relatives, stop for a week, and return through a different door.

The better question for a difficult public service is not “Can the user complete the flow?” It is “Can the person leave and come back without losing their place, dignity, or helper?”

I am collecting examples of services that support delegated help without quietly transferring all authority. Public-sector and healthcare examples are especially welcome.

## X-style post: Multilingual is an operating model

Translating the interface is the last 20% of multilingual service design.

The first 80% is deciding who answers when the translated letter creates a question, whether an interpreter can see the case history, and how a correction made in one language reaches the system of record.

## Personal post: Two cities

Berlin gives me acquaintances quickly and friendship slowly. Seoul gives me family history quickly and private time slowly.

I am trying to get better at inviting one person for tea instead of waiting for the next group dinner to do the work for me.

## Project note: A small studio, maybe

I keep circling an idea for a two- or three-person service-design studio that works on high-stakes transitions: hospital discharge, residency renewal, bereavement administration, and benefits appeals.

The model would combine research and implementation. No 120-slide strategy handoff. I would like to meet someone who has sold and delivered work inside government or health systems and still believes procurement constraints are design constraints.

## Voice markers

- Begins from lived detail, then makes a careful systems claim.
- Avoids motivational language and easy villains.
- Comfortable holding two conflicting truths.
- Asks for cases and collaborators, not engagement.
